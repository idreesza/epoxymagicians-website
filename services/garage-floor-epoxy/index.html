Why garages in Texas need a real coating system

North Texas garages take a beating: expansive clay soils shift the slab, summer heat softens cheap coatings, and hot tires can pull up a poorly bonded floor (a failure called "hot tire pickup"). A professionally installed epoxy or polyaspartic system solves all three. It resists oil, brake fluid, road salt and UV, wipes clean with a mop, and brightens the whole garage by reflecting light off a seamless surface with no grout lines or porous concrete to trap dust.

Our garage floor epoxy process

The coating is only as good as the prep. Here is exactly how we install every garage floor:

  1. Diamond grinding. We mechanically grind the entire slab with industrial diamond tooling to open the concrete's pores. We never acid-etch — grinding is the single biggest reason our floors don't peel.
  2. Crack & joint repair. Cracks, spalls and pitting are filled with a structural polymer and ground flush so the finished floor is smooth.
  3. Moisture check. We test the slab for moisture vapor, which is common on DFW slabs, and select a coating chemistry that will bond.
  4. Base coat. A high-solids epoxy or polyaspartic base coat is applied for adhesion and build.
  5. Decorative flake or metallic. Vinyl flake is broadcast to full refusal (or a metallic pigment is troweled and manipulated) to your chosen color blend.
  6. Polyaspartic topcoat. A UV-stable clear topcoat locks everything in, adds slip resistance and delivers the final gloss.

Flake vs. metallic vs. solid color

Flake (chip) floors are the most popular garage choice — durable, slip-resistant, and forgiving of minor slab imperfections, with custom color blends. Metallic floors create a high-gloss, marbled, showroom look and are ideal if your garage doubles as a display or shop. Solid-color systems are the most economical and give a clean, uniform finish. All three can be finished with a polyaspartic topcoat for UV stability and next-day parking.

How long does installation take?

Most 2-car garages are completed in one day with a polyaspartic system, or over two days with a traditional epoxy build. You can walk on the floor within a few hours and park vehicles in about 24 hours. The garage must be empty on install day so we can grind and coat the full slab.

Garage floor epoxy FAQs

How much does garage floor epoxy cost in DFW?

Flake systems run $5–$8/sq ft and metallic $8–$12/sq ft. A 2-car garage typically costs $1,800–$4,500 installed, including grinding, crack repair and topcoat.

Is epoxy or polyaspartic better for a Texas garage?

Polyaspartic is UV-stable and won't yellow in the heat, and it cures fast for a one-day install. We often use an epoxy base for build with a polyaspartic topcoat for durability.

How long does it last?

15–20+ years when properly prepped. Because we diamond-grind every slab instead of acid-etching, our coatings resist peeling and hot-tire pickup.

Do I need to empty the garage?

Yes — the slab must be clear so we can grind and coat all of it. The space is ready for your belongings in about 24 hours.
