Epoxy Flooring Cost in Dallas: 2026 Price Guide

Cost guide · Epoxy Magicians · Updated [[PUBLISH_DATE]]

Epoxy flooring costs $5–$12 per square foot installed in the Dallas–Fort Worth area in 2026, depending on the coating system and slab condition. Flake systems typically start around $5–$8/sq ft, metallic systems run $8–$12/sq ft, and commercial or driveway projects are priced individually after a walkthrough. Here's exactly what moves the number up or down.

What actually drives the price

Four factors set the final price on almost every job we quote in DFW:

  • Slab condition. Cracks, spalling, and old coatings that need to be ground off add labor before a single drop of coating goes down.
  • Coating system. Solid-color and flake epoxy are the most affordable; metallic epoxy costs more because of the hand-applied technique; polyaspartic costs a bit more per square foot than epoxy but saves labor time.
  • Square footage. Larger jobs have a lower cost per square foot since mobilization and equipment setup are fixed costs spread over more area.
  • Accessibility. A detached shop with a long gravel driveway costs more to service than an attached garage off a paved street.

Typical cost by project type in DFW

Based on the systems we install most often:

  • 2-car garage (400–500 sq ft): $1,800–$4,500 for flake or solid color; $3,200–$6,000 for metallic.
  • Driveway: $3,000–$9,000 depending on length and slab condition — see our driveway coating page for details.
  • Patio or pool deck: $2,500–$7,000 for a textured, UV-stable system.
  • Commercial/industrial: priced per project based on traffic load and chemical exposure — see commercial epoxy flooring.

Why the cheapest quote often costs more later

A quote that's dramatically below the ranges above is almost always skipping the step that matters most: diamond grinding the slab instead of acid-etching it. Acid etching is faster and cheaper to perform, but it leaves a weaker bond that commonly fails within 1–3 years — at which point the floor has to be ground off and redone anyway, so the true cost ends up higher than paying for proper prep the first time.

How we build a written estimate

Every estimate we send breaks the job into the same line items: slab prep (grinding and crack repair), base coat material and labor, decorative flake or metallic application if applicable, and topcoat. We measure the space rather than estimating from a phone description, because garage and driveway square footage varies more than most homeowners expect once you account for aprons, steps, and irregular shapes. You'll see each line item before you commit, not a single bundled number.

Cost surprises worth asking about upfront

Three things commonly change a quote after a homeowner has already budgeted for a lower number elsewhere: hidden cracks or spalling revealed once old paint or sealer is ground off, elevated moisture readings that require an upgraded primer, and control joint or expansion joint work on older slabs. Ask any installer directly whether their quote assumes a best-case slab or includes an allowance for these issues — we quote a not-to-exceed range specifically so surprises don't turn into a change order mid-job.

Comparing quotes from different installers

When comparing bids, ask exactly how each installer preps the slab. A noticeably cheaper quote is very often skipping diamond grinding in favor of acid etching, or skipping crack repair on a slab that clearly needs it. Two quotes that look similar on price can produce very different floors five years later based on that one difference alone.

Getting the most accurate quote for your project

The fastest way to get an accurate number is to send a few photos of the space along with rough dimensions when you request an estimate. Photos let us spot cracking, existing coatings, or moisture staining before we arrive, which means the written estimate you receive is close to the final price rather than a rough range that changes once we see the slab in person. For larger commercial spaces, a brief walkthrough is worth scheduling since traffic patterns and chemical exposure affect which system makes sense as much as square footage does.

FAQ

How much does a 2-car garage epoxy floor cost in Dallas?

A typical 2-car garage (400–500 sq ft) costs $1,800–$4,500 installed for flake or solid-color epoxy, or $3,200–$6,000 for a metallic system.

Does epoxy flooring cost more in 2026 than in previous years?

Material and labor costs have risen gradually across the flooring industry, but the $5–$12/sq ft range has stayed a reliable benchmark for professionally installed epoxy in DFW.

Is a cheaper epoxy quote ever a good deal?

Only if it still includes diamond grinding and proper crack repair. A quote that skips slab prep to hit a lower price usually costs more once the coating fails early and has to be redone.
